## Historiographical findings worth using in narration

1. **Saraighat.** Gait's 1906 history has no named battle, no illness story and no poppy seeds. The famous narrative was built later, mainly by S. K. Bhuyan (1947), from Buranjis edited after Gait wrote. That is a good "historian's note" for the long-form episode.
2. **The founding story includes violence against the Nagas,** and the chronicles themselves record it. Leaving it out would be a sanitisation.
3. **Material evidence** that can be shown on screen: Ramakant's coins dated 1691 Saka (1769), and the cannon inscribed 1604 Saka (1682).
4. **Shan chronicles** (via Ney Elias, cited by Gait p. 73) credit a different Mao general, Samlungpha, with campaigns in Upper Assam around 1220. Gait reads the two traditions as compatible.
